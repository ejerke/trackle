# 0022. Insane on 10×10, and a faster solution counter

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-13
- Amends: [0019](0019-insane-crossings.md)

## Context

On 8×8, Insane was uneven. The given bridge fixes four exits at once, and on
a small grid that often decided a large part of the track around it, so some
puzzles played easier than Hard. On raw 8×8 candidates, local rules alone
decided a median 53% of the grid and the median puzzle needed 3 trial steps.
On 10×10 the same numbers were 36% and 12.

A 10×10 grid is much more work for the generator, which runs on request
(daily puzzles once a day, random puzzles on every new seed). The first
10×10 attempt took up to a minute for one puzzle; the target is well under
half a second for most puzzles.

## Decision

- Insane is 10×10 (`LEVELS`).
- Filters: 42–66 track squares, tier exactly 3, at least 12 trial steps
  (Hard: 8), tier 1 decides ≤ 20%, no giveaway lines, at most 3 hints
  besides the bridge. Stricter settings (≥ 16 trial steps, ≤ 15%, ≤ 2 hints)
  played harder but more than doubled the slowest generation times.
- **Faster counting** (`countSolutions` in logic.ts), for every level:
  - When the rules get stuck, split on whether a square has track, in the
    row or column with the fewest undecided squares, before splitting on
    edges. It used to split on the first unknown edge in grid order. This is
    the big win: proving a 10×10 layout with only the bridge has several
    solutions went from up to minutes to under 0.1 s.
  - A contradiction is one shared object, not a new `Error` with a stack
    trace (~8% of all time), and the connectivity rules reuse their arrays.
  - Every branch is still searched, so counts are unchanged.
- **Uniqueness budget** (`uniquenessBudget: 1000`, Insane only): while
  choosing hints, a uniqueness check that takes more than 1000 search steps
  gives up and counts as "not unique", so the generator adds (or keeps) a
  hint. That can never let an ambiguous puzzle through. It is a safety net
  for the rare layout the better branching still finds slow.

## Alternatives considered

- **Stay on 8×8 with stricter filters**: filters can reject the puzzles the
  bridge gives away, but leave the bridge dominating a small grid.
- **Branch at the loose end of a piece of track**: the natural guess, but it
  made the search slower than grid order.
- **One global budget for every level**: 2000 steps left other levels
  unchanged on 200 seeds, but 1000 already changed some, too thin a margin
  to promise for every seed. Insane-only keeps other levels identical.
- **Looser still** (≥ 10 trial steps, ≤ 25%): barely faster (p90 446 → 420 ms).

## Consequences

- Insane puzzles for a given seed changed. Stored daily puzzles keep their
  layout; Insane random puzzles are regenerated from their seed, so an
  unfinished saved random board shows against a different puzzle (edges
  outside the grid are dropped, nothing breaks). `GENERATOR_VERSION` stays 3:
  a fingerprint of 200 Easy/Medium/Hard/Super-duper puzzles is identical, and
  generating them got ~35% faster.
- Over 200 Insane seeds: all accepted; median 29 trial steps (p10 17); tier 1
  decides a median 3%; median 3 hints including the bridge. Generation:
  median ~0.14 s, p90 ~0.45 s, max ~1.3 s. What's left is the number of
  candidates rejected (median 12 attempts), not the cost of one.
